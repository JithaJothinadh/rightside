Frequently asked questions

Right Side helps you find which side of the road to wait on for your bus or tram in Switzerland, especially if you are new here and don't speak German, French or Italian.

Why do two bus stops have the same name?

In Switzerland, one stop name usually covers several waiting points, called poles or platforms. Often there is one on each side of the street, one for each direction. A navigation app tells you the stop name, but not which of those poles your bus leaves from.

If you wait on the wrong side, the bus goes the other way, or doesn't stop for you at all.

How do I know I'm at the right pole?

Right Side gives you three checks:

  • The letter. Wait at the pole Right Side shows, for example D.
  • The sign. Every pole has a sign listing its lines and where they go. It must show your line and your destination, for example "12 → Zentrum Paul Klee". If it shows a different destination, cross to the other side. This check works even if you can't read German.
  • The street side. Where it helps, Right Side also tells you which side of which street, for example "Kramgasse, south side".
What do the letters A, B, C mean?

Where OpenStreetMap records the letter printed on the real stop sign, Right Side uses that letter. Where it doesn't, Right Side numbers the poles A, B, C itself, and then the letter may not match the sign. That's why the sign check, line and destination, is the one to trust.

Which stops are included?

Right Side is a prototype. It covers only these 8 stops, chosen because several poles there share the same name:

  • Basel, Aeschenplatz
  • Bern, Zytglogge
  • Fribourg, Tilleul/Cathédrale
  • Genève, Plainpalais
  • Lausanne, Chauderon
  • Luzern, Kantonalbank
  • Zürich, Bucheggplatz
  • Zürich, Schaffhauserplatz

Other stops are not included yet. More can be added from Swiss open transport data.

How does the AR view work?

Tap AR view and hold your phone up. The camera shows the street, a green arrow points towards your stop, and labels float over the stops as you turn, with their distance. Your phone asks once for permission to use the camera and, on iPhone, motion.

It uses your phone's GPS and compass, which in old towns can be 10–20 m and a few degrees off. So AR helps you find the right area and face the right way, but two stops facing each other across a street can look the same. Always check the letter and the sign at the stop.

Scan sign solves that: point the camera at the stop sign and tap the button. Your phone reads the sign's text (stop name, line numbers, destinations) and works out exactly which stop you are at, then shows you the way to yours. The reading happens on your phone; the photo is not sent anywhere. The first scan downloads the text-recognition software (a few MB). If the sign can't be read clearly, Right Side asks you to move closer and try again instead of guessing.

Does it show live departure times?

No. Right Side tells you where to wait, not when the bus comes. For times, use the SBB app or the timetable on the stop sign. Right Side also only covers direct journeys, not trips where you change lines.

Is my location shared or stored?

No. When you tap "Use my location", your phone works out the nearest stop inside the page itself. Your position is not sent to Right Side or anyone else, and nothing is stored. The only thing the page remembers, on your own device, is the language you picked.

The AR view uses your camera only to show the street on your screen. The picture is not recorded, saved or sent anywhere.

Why doesn't "Listen" speak my language?

Listen uses the voices installed on your phone or computer. If your device has no voice for your language, Right Side tells you and links to how to add one. Many devices have no voice for Albanian, Bosnian, Macedonian, Persian or Tamil, and almost none have Kurdish or Tigrinya yet. In those cases, read the text, or use "Show this to someone nearby".

Why is my language marked "beta"?

Kurdish, Tamil and Tigrinya have not yet been checked by a native speaker. The directions should be understandable, but some wording may be wrong. If you speak one of these languages and can help review it, please get in touch through the project page below.

Does it work without internet?

Once the page has loaded, yes. Choosing stops, the map and the directions all keep working without a connection, and so does GPS. Opening the page for the first time needs internet.

Where does the information come from?

Pole positions, routes and streets come from OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). Stop names and coordinates come from opentransportdata.swiss. The data is a snapshot, so always check the sign on site.

Something is wrong. How can I tell you?

Please report it on the project page on GitHub: which stop, which language, and what you saw. Wrong pole positions can also be fixed directly in OpenStreetMap.
